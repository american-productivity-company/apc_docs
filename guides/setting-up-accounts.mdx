title: "Dedicated Accounts for Righthands"

A dedicated account gives a Righthand its own identity and audit trail in a service. Use one when you want activity, access, and ownership separated from a manager's personal account.

## Before you begin

- Confirm that the service permits the account type and automated use you intend.
- Decide who will retain the password, recovery method, and administrator access.
- Grant the minimum organization, workspace, or repository access needed.

## Create and connect the account

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find the Righthand's contact details">
    Open [Righthands](https://teams.righthand.ai/righthands), select the Righthand, and note their email address and phone number.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the provider account">
    Sign up with the Righthand's email address or phone number when the provider permits it. Use a clear display name so teammates can recognize the Righthand's activity.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete verification">
    If the provider sends an email or text, open the Righthand's **Activity** to find the incoming message. Complete the verification in your own browser.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Secure account recovery">
    Store the password and recovery codes in a team-controlled password manager. Follow the provider's MFA policy; do not rely on the Righthand as the only recovery path.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the connection">
    Go to [Connections](https://teams.righthand.ai/connections), select **Add**, find the service, and authorize the new account.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set Righthand scope">
    Open the new connection, change **Scope** to **Righthand**, choose the Righthand, and review tool permissions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test access">
    Ask the Righthand to perform a small read-only task and confirm the result in Activity.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Do not send passwords, recovery codes, or access tokens to a Righthand through text, email, or chat. Use the platform's connection controls or the provider's authorization flow.
</Warning>
